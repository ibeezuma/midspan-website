The carrier, towns, roads, loads and rates are invented for demonstration. Costs are illustrative ($/km by truck type, $38 an hour for the driver, $120 for each truck used). The 13 hour driving and 14 hour on-duty limits are simplified and are not legal advice. A real system would add truck-specific road restrictions, live traffic, driver rest rules, pickup stops and returns, and customer service calendars.

Method: road distances and times come from shortest paths over the network, using 100, 80 and 60 km/h on highways, secondary and local roads. Each truck loads 30 minutes at the yard, serves its stops in order and returns. The planner inserts each load where it adds the least cost, then improves the plan by moving loads between trucks and reversing parts of routes until no change helps. The load-number plan gives each load, in order, to the truck carrying the least weight so far, and serves stops in that order.
